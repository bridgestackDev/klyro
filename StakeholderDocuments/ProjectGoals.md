# Klyro — Project Goals

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** May 2026
**Owner:** BridgeStack Dev
**Audience:** Founders, investors, advisors, strategic partners

---

## Mission

Give every appointment-based service business in Latin America the infrastructure to run their bookings professionally — without giving up their brand, their clients, or a cut of every transaction.

## The problem we are solving

Across Latin America, millions of small service businesses — barbershops, salons, fitness trainers, spas, tattoo studios, car washes, pet groomers — run their scheduling through WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, and phone calls.

This works at small scale but breaks down predictably:

- **Lost revenue from no-shows.** Industry data shows 5–25% of appointments end in no-shows when there is no automated reminder system.
- **Interrupted work.** Owners and staff are pulled away from clients to respond to booking messages all day.
- **No professional image.** "DM me to book" is a default for individual operators but signals amateur to higher-end clients.
- **No data ownership.** When the conversation history lives in a personal WhatsApp account, leaving means starting over.

The existing solutions don't fit:

- **Marketplace platforms** (Fresha, Booksy, Squire) take a commission on every booking, own the customer relationship, and actively promote competitors to that customer inside the same app.
- **Enterprise SaaS** (Mindbody, Vagaro) is built for chains with administrative staff. The setup takes a week. The pricing assumes you already have revenue.
- **Calendly-style tools** are built for B2B knowledge workers, not for the texture of service businesses (multiple staff, branches, walk-in dynamics, regional payment methods).

There is no product that says: *"Here is a clean, fast, mobile-first scheduling layer. It is yours. Your link, your brand, your customers, no commission."* That is the gap Klyro fills.

## Vision

In three years, Klyro is the default scheduling layer for service businesses across Latin America. A business owner anywhere from Tegucigalpa to São Paulo signs up, configures their business in fifteen minutes, and starts taking appointments through a link they share on Instagram or WhatsApp. They never see a commission line on their statement, and their customers see their brand — not ours.

## Business model

**Transparent monthly subscription.** No commission on bookings. No transactional rake. No fees that scale with the customer's success.

Tiered by usage — the smallest barbershop pays a different price than the multi-location salon — but the model is always: a fixed, predictable monthly fee in exchange for the platform.

Why this matters: when a customer succeeds, we don't extract more from them. When they hesitate, they don't pay more for marketing they didn't ask for. The incentive alignment is permanent: we make money when they save time, not when they sell more services. That is a different relationship than what marketplaces offer.

## Launch wedge

The platform supports eight verticals from day one — barbershop, salon, fitness, spa, tattoo, car wash, pet grooming, and a generic catch-all. But we are launching focused.

**Closed beta:** ten pioneer businesses in Honduras, weighted to barbershops and salons. We pick this wedge because:

- We have direct community access in Tegucigalpa and San Pedro Sula.
- These verticals share the simplest scheduling primitive (one client, one staff, one service, one slot) — minimal product complexity at launch.
- The local market is underserved by existing platforms, and the price sensitivity makes our no-commission model especially compelling.

**Day 70 decision gate:** if pioneer retention exceeds sixty percent and at least one non-wedge vertical (fitness, spa, car wash) has shown organic uptake, we open self-serve signup to all eight verticals.

## Why we win

Three structural advantages, in order of durability:

**1. Multi-vertical from day one.**
The data model and the UI both adapt to the vertical the owner picks. Adding a new vertical is a code-only change — no database migration, no schema break. Competitors built for one vertical (theCut for barbers, Mindbody for fitness) cannot retroactively become general-purpose. We can absorb their categories one by one.

**2. Spanish-first, regionally native.**
Every error message, every email, every booking page — built in Spanish first, English second. Country-aware phone validation, currency, timezone, and date formatting from day one. Most competitors translated their product. We started in Spanish and added English on top.

**3. The customer relationship belongs to the business.**
The booking page lives at `klyro.app/<their-name>`, displays their logo, and uses their tone of voice. The customer experience is theirs, not ours. We do not cross-sell. We do not show the customer "other barbers near you." This is a category we are creating in Latin America's small service economy, and we believe it is what unlocks word-of-mouth growth.

## What success looks like

**Six months from launch:**

- One hundred paying businesses across at least three verticals.
- Greater than seventy-five percent monthly retention.
- Net Promoter Score above forty among active owners.
- A reproducible self-serve onboarding flow — owners signing up without a sales call.

**Eighteen months from launch:**

- Operating in Honduras, El Salvador, Guatemala, and one larger Latin American country (Mexico or Colombia).
- One thousand active businesses.
- Profitable on a unit-economics basis at the per-business level.

**Thirty-six months from launch:**

- The default scheduling layer for the long tail of Latin American service businesses.
- A team large enough to extend the platform into adjacent categories (payments, client retention features, native mobile) without losing the core promise: your business, your clients, your brand.

## Operating principles

A few decisions we have made on purpose, because they shape everything else:

- **No marketplace, ever.** This is not a "phase one" choice. Klyro will never become a directory where customers shop across businesses. That violates the trust our customers place in us.
- **Owner-friendly pricing.** We will raise prices when we add value, not when we have leverage. Existing customers should never feel penalized for being early.
- **Sober about scope.** We say no to most feature requests, especially in the first year. The MVP exists to test one hypothesis: do owners abandon manual WhatsApp scheduling for Klyro? Everything that doesn't answer that question waits.
- **Quality is non-negotiable.** A pioneer who has a bug in their first booking will not come back. We ship slower than we could and we test things in production-equivalent conditions before users see them.

## Founding team and accountability

The product is being built by BridgeStack Dev, an engineering studio focused on Latin American market software. The team has end-to-end ownership: product, engineering, design, go-to-market for the closed beta. As we move past validation, the team will expand into customer success and regional expansion roles.

## What we ask of partners and investors

The first phase of Klyro is bootstrapped to validation. We are looking for partners — not transactional capital — who:

- Understand the Latin American service economy and can help us reach the long tail efficiently.
- Have built or invested in vertical SaaS and recognize the difference between marketplace and infrastructure business models.
- Can offer counsel on the timing of geographic expansion and the operational shape of a multi-country SaaS.

We will raise capital when retention and unit economics tell us the model is working. Until then, we are heads-down on the product and the pioneer cohort.

## Closing

Klyro is not the most ambitious software project being built today. It is a focused product solving a clear problem for a specific market, with a clear path from validation to scale. The bet is that disciplined execution against a real problem beats grand strategy against an imagined one.

We are building it.
